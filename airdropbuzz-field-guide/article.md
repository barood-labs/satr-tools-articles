# Using AirdropBuzz as a Filter, Not a Shopping List: A Bilingual (EN/عربي) Field Guide

*By Satr Tools*

Most airdrop sites are really lists that try to get you excited. AirdropBuzz is useful for a different reason: it puts the boring facts right next to the hype. Those facts are what it costs to take part, whether the reward is confirmed, the snapshot date, and a score that the site itself calls "an editorial signal, not a safety rating." This guide shows how I use it as a **filter**, so that I end up joining fewer campaigns and better ones. There's a short Arabic summary at the end for Arabic-speaking readers, who are underserved in airdrop education.

## 1. Read the cost tag before the score
Each card on the homepage carries a cost label: **100% Free**, **Capital Required** or **Cost Unknown**. On Oct 18, 2026 the top five cards were Axis Robotics Kaito, NEAR, AlloX, Amadeus and TastyCo. Three of them had scores of 88, yet only one of the five was tagged 100% Free. A high score means the opportunity looks worth watching. It does **not** mean it's cheap or safe. My rule: filter by cost first, then sort by score.

## 2. Separate "Confirmed" from "Points Live"
A **Confirmed** tag means a token distribution has actually been announced. NEAR's card, for example, reports live allocations and notes that AirdropBuzz itself received 4.22 NEAR. **Points Live** means you're earning points that *may* become tokens. TxFlow's guide says plainly that a future token airdrop "remains unconfirmed." If you're spending time or money, that one tag changes the math.

## 3. Use "What Changed" as your daily 2-minute check
The **Airdrop Updates** feed logs material edits to live guides, such as snapshot dates, claim openings and points-rule changes. Most missed airdrops are missed because of timing, not lack of information. On Oct 18 the feed flagged a MINT snapshot and the Axis Kaito Epoch 2 cutoff at 12:00 UTC. Checking it once a day replaces following dozens of project accounts.

## 4. Run unknown links through the AI Risk Checker before anything touches your wallet
With a free account you can paste a link into the **AI Risk Checker**. It looks for suspicious domains, wallet-connection risks, requests for your seed phrase, deposit requirements and unrealistic rewards. It isn't a guarantee, and AirdropBuzz says so itself. But it's a good speed bump before the riskiest moment, which is connecting a wallet or signing something. My own rules stay the same no matter what any tool says: never share a seed phrase, never pay to "claim," and use a separate empty wallet for experiments.

## 5. Treat Market Pulse as context, not signals to trade on
**Market Pulse** tracks crypto signals with fixed rules and keeps an archive. I use it to understand why a token or sector is getting attention around a TGE, not as a buy button.

## 6. Learn from the "Recently ended" archive
The archive of ended campaigns, such as Arction, TrendBTC and CZR, shows how campaigns actually wind down and how long they run. That's the best way I know to calibrate expectations for the next one.

## My 5-step routine
1. Open the homepage and filter mentally for **100% Free**.
2. Keep only **Confirmed** campaigns, or Points Live ones where my effort is tiny.
3. Check **What Changed** for deadlines in the next 72 hours.
4. Run every new link through the **AI Risk Checker**.
5. Save keepers to the dashboard watchlist and use the **Task Planner** checklist.

## What AirdropBuzz doesn't do
It doesn't guarantee rewards, TGE dates or safety, and it labels sponsored placements as paid. I trust a research site more when it says what it can't do.
